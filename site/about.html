How we measure squad freshness and familiarity

Squads Methodology

What this is

Two complementary angles on World Cup 2026 squad readiness: freshness — how much competitive football each player has played in the 365 days before the tournament — and familiarity — how much time team-mates have spent on the pitch together over the past three seasons.

The idea is borrowed from football analytics: a squad full of match-sharp players who have repeatedly played alongside each other is, all else equal, better prepared than one whose stars arrive carrying injury time or have never shared a club dressing room.

Freshness

For each player we collect every competitive appearance recorded on Transfermarkt in the 365-day window ending on the day data was collected (June 2026). Freshness is the total minutes played in that window.

Players showing "no data in window" fall into one of two categories:

  • Genuinely inactive — long-term injury or not registered for the full window (e.g. Lucas Hernandez, ACL).
  • API coverage gap — Transfermarkt's match-report API only covers games for which TM has published a detailed statistical report. Certain competitions are absent or partially covered: Saudi Pro League, Egyptian Premier League, Iranian Pro League, and some smaller CONCACAF leagues. Players based in these leagues will show lower minute totals than their actual playing time warrants. Roughly 2% of squad members have no coverage in the 12-month window.

Familiarity network

For every pair of players within a squad we count the minutes they were simultaneously on the pitch in the same match across the three most recent full seasons: 2023/24, 2024/25, and 2025/26.

The raw co-play minutes are then weighted by recency: the current season (2025/26) counts at full weight, last season at 0.7×, and the season before at 0.5×. This reflects the reality that team chemistry built last month matters more than games played two years ago.

The result is an undirected weighted graph — a familiarity network — for each squad. Edge thickness and opacity in the visualisation encode weighted co-play minutes.

Network metrics

MetricDefinition
Density Share of all possible edges that actually exist (i.e. pairs who have played together at least once). A squad of 26 has 325 possible pairs; density = edges / 325. Higher = more players with shared experience.
Clustering Average local clustering coefficient — how often a player's team-mates also play with each other. High clustering indicates tight sub-groups (e.g. a dominant club providing several starters).

Data sources

SourceUsed for

Limitations

  • Club-only appearances — international matches are included only if Transfermarkt records them under the player's profile (most are). Friendly appearances are excluded.
  • Co-play at club level only — two players who have only faced each other as opponents, or who played together at international level but never at the same club, will show no edge. Club familiarity is the dominant signal.
  • No tactical context — minutes together ≠ minutes playing in the same system or same positions. A full-back and striker may log 2,000 co-play minutes without ever meaningfully interacting on the pitch.
  • Transfermarkt coverage gaps — some leagues (Saudi Pro League, Egyptian Premier League, certain CONCACAF leagues) have incomplete or no minute-level data in the window.
